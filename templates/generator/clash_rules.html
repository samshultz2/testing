Help

Subject Clash Rules

Use these rules when two subjects must NEVER be scheduled at the same time. This is typically used for combined classes where different student groups take different subjects simultaneously.

Example: If SSS3 Iris has both Arts and Commercial students, Geography (Commercial) and Literature (Arts) happen at the same time. Create a rule so they don't clash with Literature in other classes.

Combined Class Teacher Rules

Use these rules when a teacher is effectively teaching during another subject's time slot (shadowing). This ensures the teacher doesn't get 3 consecutive periods when counting both their own periods and the shadowed periods.

Example: During Geography for SSS3 Iris, the Literature teacher is teaching Arts students. Add a rule so she doesn't get 3 consecutive periods.

Co-Schedule Rules

Use these rules to force two subjects to always happen at the exact same time — the opposite of a Clash Rule. Typical for a combined class that splits into two streams for electives: pairing each stream's subject keeps both streams in class together instead of one being idle while the other has a lesson.

Example: SSS2 Daisy (Arts) takes Literature while SSS2 Iris (Commercial) takes Accounting — pair them so every time one is scheduled, the other is scheduled at the same slot. Teachers of both subjects are automatically kept free of other classes at that time — no extra rule needed for that.

Day-Separation Rules

Use these rules to keep a subject off two specific days of the week together, for one class. If it lands on either day, it can't also land on the other that same week — it's still free on every other day, and free to be on just one of the two.

Example: SSS1 Rose's Physics can be on Monday or on Friday, but never both in the same week. This doesn't reduce the subject's periods/week, it only rules out that one day-pairing — so it very rarely makes a timetable infeasible, unless that subject already needs a period on every single day of the week for that class.

Period-Placement Rules

Use these rules to control where a subject can land in the day, for one class (optionally one specific arm) — more granular than the per-class "Restrictions" on the Class Subjects page, which apply to every arm of that class at once. Six rule types:

  • Always a particular period — pins the subject to one exact period, whatever day it lands on (e.g. always period 2).
  • Not the first / not the last period — the mirror of "always a particular period": rules ONE period out instead of pinning the subject into one.
  • Morning only / Afternoon only — keeps the subject entirely before or entirely after the school's configured break.
  • A range of periods — confines the subject to a window (e.g. periods 2 through 4), any day.

Example: SSS2 Lily's Chemistry must always be period 2 — Monday period 2, Wednesday period 2, whichever days it's scheduled, never any other period. Or SSS1 Rose's PE must land somewhere between periods 2 and 4. None of these change the subject's periods/week, just where in the day each one is allowed to fall — but a tight rule (a single fixed period, or a narrow range) on a subject that needs more periods/week than the class has available days can make the timetable infeasible, since each day can only offer so many matching slots.
